ID 30

Grooves ready

Multi-genre MIDI drum grooves with feel and drummer personas

Faders

  • Main — Density (extra hits)
  • Alt — Genre scrub (shared axis with Chord Vamp)
  • Third — Feel (capped by Swing max %)

Genres

  • Dub → Disco → House → Techno → Trip-Hop → Hip-Hop → Jungle → UK Garage → Dubstep
  • LEDs: continuous open red→yellow→green→cyan→blue spectrum (same as Chord Vamp)
  • Sound morphs continuously between neighboring genres along the axis

Drummers

  • Elvin → Purdie → Clyde → Jaki → Sly → Allen → Pretty Tony → Dilla
  • Long press cycles; the button flashes a distinct colour per drummer
  • The persona reshapes the genre pattern after it is read: shuffle, ghost density, backbeat lock, tom dispersal, clave rotation, roll rate, push or pull, limb drift and a machine amount
  • All of it rides on Feel — at Feel 0 every drummer plays the plain genre grid, and the personality opens up as Feel rises
  • Sly sits on the same index as Bassment’s Robbie, so the pair lines up across both apps

Buttons

  • Short — Mute (notes off); unmuting also resets to the downbeat
  • Shift+Tap — Genre fill until next downbeat (break at high density)
  • Shift+Hold — Drum solo while held: 4-bar phrase (build → busier → peak → turnaround); resolves on release
  • Long — Next drummer (same gesture as Bassment’s bassist)

Jack params

  • Jack: CV Out Any 0-10V · CV Out Any -5-5V · CV Out Stacked 0-10V · CV Out Stacked -5-5V · CV In Density · CV In Feel · CV In Reset
  • Duty and range share one param, so the sixteenth slot is free for the Drummer; stored layouts keep the duty and range they had
  • Stacked sums families — hats 1, snare 2, kick 4 of 10 units; Open Hat counts as hats, Clap as snare, toms as kick
  • The jack follows the groove, so it still pulses with every note switched off
  • CV Att

Params / LEDs

  • Notes: Kick, Snare, Hats, Open Hat, Low Tom, High Tom, Clap — note 0 switches a voice off
  • MIDI Ch is the base for all seven; Ch Map overrides it per voice (7 packed nibbles, 0 = follow base)
  • Groove, Drummer, Swing max % (negative = reverse swing), GATE %, MIDI Out
  • Groove = pocket: 0 is dead on the grid; up adds per-family ms drift (kick tight → hats loosest), velocity bow and walking ghosts
  • GATE % is per voice — closed hats choke inside their step, open hats and toms may ring past it; CV pulse stays inside the 16th
  • Button Main: genre hue sat half→full (density) + bar lightness; hit dips kick −50% / snare −30% / hats −10%
  • Button Third: White→Color→Red (swing); same voice-weighted hit dips
  • Button Alt: committed genre hue (Top/Bottom = live fader meters; color changes only after latch pickup)

Manual. Genre MIDI drums on the global clock, sharing its nine genres with Chord Vamp and Bassment; the genre scrub morphs continuously between neighbours instead of stepping. Groove is the pocket: at zero every voice sits exactly on the grid, so Grooves locks tight against other Faderpunk apps. Turning it up drifts each family by its own few milliseconds — kick tightest, hats widest, scaled by the genre — bows velocity across the bar and walks which ghost hits Density reveals, so repeated bars stop reading identically. At high Density, sparse doubles, flams, triplets and rolls layer onto written kick/snare/hat hits (kick stays conservative; hats genre-typical); sub-strokes land inside the same 16th after swing/microtiming, never re-applying global swing. Shift+Tap plays a genre fill that crescendos toward the bar line independent of Density; above roughly 60 % Density it turns subtractive and the floor drops out for a break, handed back on the one by a late snare pickup. Shift+Hold escalates into a four-bar solo — build, busier bar, peak, turnaround — with accents pinned to the one and the backbeat, so Feel adds jitter instead of randomising the dynamics. Each genre carries three fill, break and solo figures; low Feel locks to one, higher Feel rolls between them. Clap, Open Hat and the toms are optional layers derived from hits already in the groove and stay silent until you give them a note: Open Hat replaces an accented offbeat hat rather than doubling it, and the toms only appear in the back half of a fill, break or solo. On top of the genre sits a drummer: eight personas that reshape the written pattern the way Bassment’s bassists reshape a bassline, cycled with a long press. Purdie pulls the grid into a halftime shuffle and fills the gaps with ghosts while the backbeat stays nailed down; Jaki plays it dead straight and quantised; Pretty Tony rotates the kick off the downbeat into a clave and tumbles the toms; Dilla drifts the hats against a kick that sits behind the beat. Elvin, Clyde, Sly and Allen sit between those poles with their own mix of triplets, sixteenth chatter, steppers weight and afrobeat scatter. Because every persona trait is scaled by Feel, a flat Feel still gives the plain genre grid no matter who is behind the kit, and the character arrives as you open Feel up — so the drummer is a second dimension on the same fader, not a preset switch.
