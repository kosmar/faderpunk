Ripppple is a chain of LFOs rather than four independent ones. Channel 0 is the root: its jack is a CV input, and if nothing is patched it falls back to an internal morphing LFO after about a second of a static signal. Channel 1 is modulated by the root, channel 2 by the output of channel 1, and channel 3 by the output of channel 2. Every stage only ever sees its direct predecessor, so what starts as a tidy relationship at the front of the chain diverges further and further towards the back — small moves on the root fan out into behaviour the last output can’t easily be read back from.

Each stage decides for itself what the incoming signal does to it. A long press on channels 1–3 cycles the modulation target through Rate, Depth and Shape: Rate bends the tempo of that stage exponentially over roughly ±3 octaves, Depth is straight amplitude modulation that ducks the stage towards silence as its predecessor swings negative, and Shape pushes the wave along the morph axis. The faders carry three layers — the plain fader sets the root speed on channel 0 and the modulation depth on the stages, Shift reaches morph and base rate, and holding a channel’s own button reaches skew, that stage’s morph, and — on channel 3 only — the symmetry shared by the whole chain. Channel 0’s button freezes the root value (the value itself, so stepped morph nodes hold too), Shift plus a tap puts the internal LFO on clock sync, a short tap on channels 1–3 mutes them with a click-free ramp, and Shift plus a long press cycles that stage’s output range.

The LEDs meter the chain rather than merely decorate it. Top and bottom form a bipolar display of each stage’s output, and the hue of channels 1–3 encodes the active modulation target, so a glance tells you which stage is being rate-bent and which is being ducked. Channel 0 switches hue depending on whether the CV input or the internal LFO is driving it, which makes the auto fallback visible instead of mysterious. Muted channels drop to a heavy dim and their button LED goes dark, and the button on channel 0 stays bright while the chain is frozen. All four channels can mirror their level as MIDI CC or NRPN, using the base CC plus the channel offset.
